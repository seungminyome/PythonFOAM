PythonFOAM: a vision-language observer for OpenFOAM runs

An optional observer for the PODFoam example of PythonFOAM: during an OpenFOAM run the solver hands its state to Python, which draws |U| and p and asks a vision-language model served locally by llama.cpp to describe the pictures, either while the solver waits or while it keeps stepping. The answers are recorded, and nothing from the model goes back into the solver; the repository README describes how to run it.

The two v2 pictures the running solver sent to Gemma 4 12B at step 250 of a developed backward-facing-step flow (t = 0.10125 s): |U| and p, each on its own colour scale, plotted range x 0 to 0.12 m, y 0 to 0.04 m.

Results

The repository measures how well such models read solver fields, what the requests cost the run, and how the answers compare with simple numbers computed from the arrays.

A position counts as right when it lies within 10 % of the plotted range of the true cell. The constant baseline is the score of the best single answer given to every picture. v1 is the solver's original picture, |U| above p in two panels with axes in metres; v2, shown above, draws one field per picture, with axes in percent of the plotted range and a grid line every 10 %; v2own is v2 with each picture of a sequence on its own colour scale. Unless noted, the case is a 2D laminar flow over a backward-facing step.

With v2 pictures every model in the table beat the constant baseline in every position test, and Gemma 4 12B did so from a running solver over 24,000 steps. Most fastest-flow misses in the developed flow name another spot with at least 90 % of the largest speed. Over the 24,000-step run the answered lowest-pressure position follows the spot as it moves between vortex cores, with a correlation of 0.78 in x. With the solver's v1 pictures no model did clearly better than the constant baseline in the trend test.

As 4-bit files with the image-token setting llama.cpp recommends for them, Qwen3.5-35B-A3B and 27B placed the fastest flow of the developed flow in 133 and 128 of 140 pictures, more often than any Gemma model; the two model families ran with different files and settings. On a second geometry, the laminar flow past a cylinder (offsetCylinder), the three Gemma models placed the fastest flow in 32 to 39 of 40 windows against a constant baseline of 8; the lowest pressure, a small spot beside a large dark wake, 31B found in 38 and 12B in 18. Gemma 4 E2B, the model of the CPU setup, placed an isolated fastest-flow spot in 4 of 32 synthetic v1 pictures and 11 of 32 v2 pictures, against a constant baseline of 5.

In the instability test every model said unstable for the triples that end one step before a crash, where the field has already blown up; none warned more than 5 steps ahead. A rule on the peak |U| growth does as well as the best model, and one on the cell-to-cell jump does better.

In the wrong-setup test 31B's lead over the rule rests on two runs. On the viscosity run it is right for 8 of 8, with reasons that do not name viscosity and deny a recirculation that is there; the moving wall's change is not in the pictures. Its catches of the low inlet speed compare the colour bar's maximum with the stated speed, as the rule does. The viscosity run is the only candidate that models notice and no number needing no reference run flags, and it is weak: one run, with nearly alike frames. The largest Courant number of a whole run above 1 flags three of the five wrong runs (64 of 80), and given a normal run the four numbers at the ends of the colour bars get 75 of 80 (68 with each normal run held out).

Against upstream's PODFoam on OpenFOAM 8, U, p and phi are byte-identical. POD modes differ, because upstream's first snapshot of each window is overwritten by the next one; with that overwrite reproduced in Python, all modes equal upstream's (30 of 30 on the shipped case, 150 of 150 on the stabilised one). On openfoam.com the same holds with upstream's pressure line; this fork's line solves p as that version's pimpleFoam does, a difference of 847 of 5,142 m²/s² in p after 2×10⁻⁶ s and 31.8 of 3,456 after 2×10⁻⁵ s.

Limitations

  • Gemma 4 E2B does not give usable answers. Gemma 4 12B misreads most peak-speed changes under 2 % and misses a small pressure extreme next to a large region of similar colour, such as the cylinder's; the other models tested also miss clear speed changes. Only positions and trend words are scored; the descriptions are not.
  • The instability set has a flaw: each request states the frames' times, and the time between frames alone separates the two classes.
  • The same request on the same node gives the same answer. On another GPU the text and some positions change, but not the number of positions within 10 %.
  • Tested on 2D laminar flows and one extruded 3D case, where no model has read a slice picture; on 2 MPI ranks without a model request; with up to two requests in flight.
  • No suite with a model ran on OpenFOAM v2012 or 8: each had one 12B request, and the suites there ran against a stand-in server that reads no picture. Why OpenFOAM 8's fields differ from v2212's was not examined.
  • The wrong-setup test has one case and five wrong runs, its normal runs are pieces of one trajectory, and its reasons were judged by one AI agent (of 37, 14 name the change, 17 partly, 6 no).
  • Each request's record is written by a forked writer with a 0.2 s budget, so that the solver never waits longer. In the run with two requests in flight, one of 118 answered requests has no record.
