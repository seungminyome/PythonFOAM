# November 2: the PR branches built and validated on four OpenFOAM versions, and more model runs

MedicineBow cluster, 2026-11-01 to 2026-11-03 · NVIDIA A30, NVIDIA L40S and CPU nodes · llama.cpp b11429, commit `d812350` · Slurm jobs and code: [Records](#records)

[README](../README.md) · Evidence: [round 2](evidence/2026-11-02-round2/README.md) · Later: [November 3](results-20261103.md) · Earlier: [October 30](results-20261030.md) · [Korean](https://github.com/seungminyome/PythonFOAM-Korean/blob/main/docs/results-20261102.md)

In short: the two PR branches build and pass their checks on all four OpenFOAM
versions. PODFoam's snapshot bug also sat in three sibling solvers, and the same one-line
fix removes it there. On openfoam.com the PR branches now solve pressure as
`pimpleFoam` does. On the model side, Gemma 4 12B beats the constant baseline on random
windows of the 3D case's slices. The instability test gives the same answers when the
request holds no step or time.

The two PR branches build at every commit on OpenFOAM v2012, v2106, v2212 and 8.
The bridge validation passes on all four versions and in the original project's Docker image.
`pr/podfoam-fix` carries the snapshot fix and the validation. `pr/podfoam-observer`
adds the hooks and the observer example.

On openfoam.com, with one outer corrector, a plain `pEqn.solve()` uses the `pFinal`
solver for every pressure solve of a time step. `pimpleFoam` uses it only for the
last solve, and the PR branches now do the same. On OpenFOAM 8 the plain call already
behaves like `pimpleFoam`.

At `6fde869`, `PODFoam_Debug`, `APMOSFoam` and `AEFoam` kept a stale first snapshot in
every SVD window, as PODFoam did. A one-line owned copy, the core of PODFoam's change,
removes it in all three and leaves U and p unchanged.

OpenFOAM 8 differs from v2212 because it applies the case's `relaxationFactors` with
one outer corrector and openfoam.com does not. Plain `pimpleFoam` shows the same
difference, so PODFoam plays no part in it.

12B beat the constant baseline for both positions in 48 random windows of the
3D case's slice frames, drawn after the runs. On the 24 frames the solver drew, the
baseline was right every time. The four slices put the fastest flow and the lowest
pressure at most 1.8 % of the plotted range apart. The test shows that the slice path works
with a model, but not that the model reads changes along z.

With no step or time anywhere in the request, each model answered 69 of the 70
October 28 triples as it did on October 28. The earlier negative result did not rest
on the stated times.

The PR tree of October 30 is `bf7a06e`, and most round-2 runs used it. The original
PODFoam is [argonne-lcf/PythonFOAM](https://github.com/argonne-lcf/PythonFOAM) at
`6fde869`. The November tree is `276ea8e`, an earlier local version of the observer
branch from November 2. A position is right when it lies within 10 % of the plotted
range of the true cell. The constant baseline is the best single answer for a whole
set.

## The pressure solver on openfoam.com

The stabilised validation case ran for two steps with `p` solved by PCG/DIC and
`pFinal` by GAMG. Each `Solving for p` line then names the dictionary in use. All 30
runs exited 0 (job 25080017).

| Pressure solves per step, `nOuterCorrectors` 1 | `pimpleFoam` of the image | The PR tree | Plain `pEqn.solve()`, as in the original PODFoam |
|---|---|---|---|
| v2012, v2106, v2212 | DICPCG ×5, GAMG | DICPCG ×5, GAMG | GAMG ×6 |
| 8 | DICPCG ×5, GAMG | DICPCG ×5, GAMG | DICPCG ×5, GAMG |

- On openfoam.com `pimpleControl::loop()` sets the mesh flag `finalIteration` for the
  whole last outer iteration. `fvMatrix::solve()` picks the dictionary from that flag,
  so with one outer corrector every plain solve of the step is a final one.
- `pimpleFoam/pEqn.H` passes `p.select(pimple.finalInnerIter())` instead. That is true
  only at the last non-orthogonal iteration of the last corrector.
- OpenFOAM 8 sets the flag again before every PISO and non-orthogonal iteration, in
  `singleRegionSolutionControl::updateFinal()`. Its plain `pEqn.solve()` already
  picks `pFinal` only at the last solve.
- The `Solving for` lines of the PR tree and of `pimpleFoam` agree line for line on
  v2106. With two outer correctors both give DICPCG ×5 and GAMG in each outer
  iteration on all four versions.
- The source lines and their checksums against the images are in
  [source-excerpts.md](evidence/2026-11-02-round2/pfinal/source-excerpts.md) and
  [verify-20261102.txt](evidence/2026-11-02-round2/pfinal/verify-20261102.txt).

## Why OpenFOAM 8 differs from v2212

The difference between the two versions lies in OpenFOAM, not in PODFoam. Plain
`pimpleFoam` from the 0.1 s start state reproduces the step-100 frames of the
October 30 PODFoam runs to the 12 significant digits it writes, in 2D and 3D on both
versions. The largest differences are 5×10⁻¹¹ m/s in U and 5×10⁻¹⁰ m²/s² in p.

The case's `fvSolution` has `relaxationFactors { fields { p 0.9; } equations { U 0.9;
nuTilda 0.9; } }` and `nOuterCorrectors` 1. With one outer corrector every iteration is
the final one. v2212 looks up only `pFinal` and `UFinal`, finds neither and no
`default`, and relaxes nothing. OpenFOAM 8 falls back to `p` and `U` when the `Final`
entry is missing, and relaxes by 0.9. Neither version prints a warning. The source
lines are in [source-excerpts-relax.txt](evidence/2026-11-02-round2/of8-cause/source-excerpts-relax.txt).

In the first two rows the second number is the peak |U| or the range of p on
OpenFOAM 8.

| Step 100 | max \|ΔU\|, m/s | max \|Δp\|, m²/s² |
|---|---:|---:|
| OpenFOAM 8 against v2212, same dictionary: 2D | 3.11 of 36.9 | 23.2 of 369 |
| The same, 3D, full mesh | 3.18 of 36.9 | 23.5 of 416 |
| OpenFOAM 8 without the block against v2212: 2D | 0.0000 | 0.0001 |
| The same, 3D | 0.0001 | 0.0005 |
| v2212 with `pFinal 0.9` and `UFinal 0.9` against OpenFOAM 8: 2D | 0.0000 | 0.0001 |
| OpenFOAM 8 with the equation factors only, `U` and `nuTilda`, against v2212: 2D | 3.11 | 23.2 |
| OpenFOAM 8 with the field factor only, `p`, against v2212: 2D | 0.0093 | 0.105 |
| One step with no relaxation in either, OpenFOAM 8 against v2212: 2D | 1.0×10⁻¹⁰ | 5.0×10⁻⁹ |

- The factor on the U equation carries the difference. `nuTilda` is not solved, since
  the case is laminar.
- v2212 gives the same fields with and without the block. So does OpenFOAM 8 with and
  without explicit `Final` entries.
- In 3D OpenFOAM 8 needs 67.9 pressure iterations per solve with the block and 24.4
  without it, as v2212 does. These are means over the first 100 steps.
- The October 30 report gives the means over all 110 steps, 64.0 and 23.5.
- `fvSchemes` and `turbulenceProperties` are identical between the two versions' cases.
  `controlDict` differs only by a `libs` line on v2212.
- `Run_Case` keeps the block, so results are compared within one version.

## The flaky unit test

`test_connection_refused_is_reported_without_a_stall` in `test_async.py` checked that
step 10 stays short while the worker's connection is refused. Before the worker
connects, it draws the step's first picture. At that moment `pod_image.render()`
imported `scipy.spatial`, which took 0.86 of the 1.41 s of the first render.

Under load the first render took seconds. Step 10 then overran its bound, or the
deadline passed first and the status read `timeout`. These were the two failures of
October 30.

- The fix: `pod_image.prepare_backend` imports `scipy.spatial` at start-up, so a

- The test reads the refusal from the record: `transport_phase` connect,
  `request_sent` false and `worker_reaped` true.
- The other tests of the module had tight margins of their own, from short deadlines
  and request windows. The November tree widens them, and from then on every loaded
  run of the whole module passed.

## Limitations

- The pressure demo ran the stabilised case for two steps with one and two outer
  correctors; the shipped `Run_Case`, `finalOnLastPimpleIterOnly` and
  `residualControl` were not run.
- On OpenFOAM 8 there is no build with the plain pressure line, since the PR tree's
  `#else` branch is that line.
- The sibling solvers ran only on OpenFOAM 8 and v2106, on v2106 only with the
  stabilised case; on v2012 and v2212 they were built, not run.
- No `AEFoam` run completed, and the instruction where it crashes was not found,
  since the image has no debugger.
- The number of NaN POD files the original build wrote varied between runs and
  versions, from 5 to 9, and was not examined.
- The OpenFOAM 8 variants C to F ran in 2D only, and v2012, v2106 and the 2-rank cases
  were not rerun with plain `pimpleFoam`.
- The profile of the flaky test's first render comes from one login node with Python
  3.12.
- The 2-rank request used the 2D case on v2212 and OpenFOAM 8 only, with an A30.
- The validation's module `pod_svd.py` accepts one rank only and was edited for the
  2-rank runs.
- The v2012 and OpenFOAM 8 suites ran at `c82c835`, whose job script differs from
  `bf7a06e`'s.
- The 6,000-step default ran on v2212 only, and its frames at steps 3000 to 3750 have
  no earlier run to compare with.
- `setup_12b.sh` fetched the files once, and the prebuilt binaries ran nowhere here,
  since no node has glibc 2.38.
- `LLAMA_CUDA=13.4` was not fetched, and only a text request went to the built
  server.
- A flow that varies along z would need a much longer 3D run than the slice test had.
- The solver drew the 24 slice frames, and the 48 random windows were drawn
  afterwards from their arrays. All 72 went to the model after the runs, not as
  requests from inside the solver.
- In the instability test all 10 scored crash-bound items come from one run and share
  frames.
- The colour-bar maxima and the look of the two schemes still differ between the
  instability classes.
- Only Gemma 4 12B and 31B, and only a frame gap of 2×10⁻³ s, were tried in the
  instability test.
- The early instability items cannot be scored, since their run may be unstable from
  its start.
- The final branches ran the 3D slice serially, at one slice height and on no wedge
  mesh.
- For the final branches the GPU suite and the 2-rank request ran on v2212 only.
- No build used `-Wall` or the headers of Python 3.12.
