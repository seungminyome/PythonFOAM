# November 3: wrong-setup test 2

MedicineBow cluster, 2026-11-03 · NVIDIA RTX A6000 and CPU nodes · llama.cpp b11429, commit `d812350` · Slurm jobs and code: [Records](#records)

[README](../README.md) · Evidence: [wrong-setup test 2](evidence/2026-11-03-wrong-setup-2/README.md) · Earlier: [November 2](results-20261102.md) · [Korean](https://github.com/seungminyome/PythonFOAM-Korean/blob/main/docs/results-20261103.md)

The October 30 test asked whether a vision-language model can see that a run was set up
wrong. The lead of Gemma 4 31B over a rule there rested on two runs. This test asks the
question again with a design fixed before any request. It has 29 pairs of `pimpleFoam`
runs of the step case. Each pair is one base setup drawn at random, run as written and
with one change. Each run gives three states, and the baselines read the same pictures.

On the last state of each run, with the intended setup stated, 31B is right for 44 of 58
runs and the baseline with most right answers for 45. That baseline is a logistic regression on five numbers from the colour bars and the note: the |U| ends divided by the stated speed, the p ends divided by its square, and the logarithm of the stated viscosity.
The two disagree on 27 runs, 13 for the model and 14 for the baseline; the exact McNemar
test gives p = 1. The plan's criterion, p < 0.05, is not met: that the model does better
than the rules is not shown.

The model and the baseline are right on different runs. The model catches 26 of the 29
wrong runs and the baseline 17. The model answers `inconsistent` on 11 of the 29 normal
runs, the baseline on one. Nine of the eleven false alarms say the picture shows no
backward-facing step. The window begins at the step's corner, so the step's face lies on
the left edge and no picture shows it as an area.

Without the stated numbers the model answers `inconsistent` on 51 of 58 frames and is
right for 28, one below the constant baseline, which answers `consistent` on every run
and gets 29. Temperature 0.7 changes 10 of the 58
stated answers in each of its two variants and lowers the count to 34 and 38.

## Design

- **Runs.** 30 base setups drawn at random with a fixed seed: inlet speed 20 to 40 m/s, kinematic viscosity
  1.0×10⁻⁵ to 2.5×10⁻⁵ m²/s, a start state of the developed flow at t = 0.06 to 0.1 s.
  Each base ran as written and with one change: inlet speed ×0.1 to ×1.5 (8), viscosity
  ×0.1 to ×1000 (9), `lowerWall` or both walls `slip` (7), the inlet profile, the lowest
  quarter, half or three quarters of the inlet faces at velocity 0 (6). The ×1000 viscosity run diverged,
  and its pair was dropped as the plan says: 29 pairs, 58 runs. A pilot of 13 other runs
  showed that a tilted inlet and a uniform start state leave no visible trace in the
  plotted window, so those kinds were dropped before the design was fixed.
- **Frames.** Steps 6000, 7000 and 8000 of each run, the two pictures, |U| and p, each on
  its own colour scale, drawn in the solver's window. Step 8000 is the primary frame; one run is one sample.
- **Request.** The system prompt describes the case and asks whether the pictures are
  consistent with it, with a `reason`, an `assessment` and a `cause`. Each request carries
  the two pictures, the system prompt and a short text note. Stated: the note gives the
  base's inlet speed and viscosity, the same text for both runs of a pair.
  Blind: it does not. Temperature 0 with one seed, and 0.7 with two seeds: six sets,
  1,044 requests, all to Gemma 4 31B (QAT Q4_0).
- **Scoring.** A normal run answered `consistent` or a wrong run `inconsistent` is right;
  `cannot_tell` is wrong on both. Right for the right reason adds that the cause is the
  run's own. Exact McNemar test against the baseline with most right answers, a bootstrap
  over pairs
  for the interval, and p < 0.05 in the stated condition at temperature 0 as the
  criterion.
- **Baselines** on the same pictures and note, without a reference run: constant; the
  peak rule of October 30, which calls a run wrong when the |U| colour bar's maximum lies
  outside 1 to 2 times the stated speed; logistic regression with leave-one-pair-out on
  five numbers, the |U| colour-bar ends divided by the stated speed, the p ends divided by
  its square and the logarithm of the stated viscosity, on those plus three pixel shares,
  and on the pixel shares alone. The pixel shares are the share of slow flow in the |U|
  picture, how far that flow reaches along the lowest quarter, and the share of low
  values in the p picture.
- The request text cannot separate the classes: within a pair it is the same. The plan,
  the request set and the baselines were committed before the GPU job
  ([evidence](evidence/2026-11-03-wrong-setup-2/README.md)).

In the blind column only the constant and the pixels-only rows see what the model sees.
The other baselines read the stated numbers in both conditions, so their counts repeat,
and the peak rule needs the stated speed. The 95 % interval of the model's share at
temperature 0 is 0.67 to 0.84, the colour-bar regression's 0.67 to 0.88.

- **Right for the right reason**, stated, temperature 0: 27 of 58, the 18 normal runs and
  9 wrong runs (speed 7, walls 2). No viscosity run gets the cause `viscosity`; the model
  names walls, other, inlet speed or inlet profile. All six profile runs get `inlet_speed`,
  with the colour bar's maximum below the stated speed as the reason, which is the peak
  rule's comparison.
- **The false alarms.** Nine of the eleven reasons on normal runs say the picture shows
  no backward-facing step: "no step geometry (white area)", "no sudden expansion or
  separation zone". One sees checkerboarding in p, and one says only that the separation
  zone is missing.
  The sentence about the missing step also catches the inlet speed ×1.25 run. The 18 normal runs answered
  `consistent` all name a separation or recirculation zone behind a step.
- **Slip walls.** Three of the seven runs are answered `consistent` with "a recirculation
  zone and reattachment after a step". The colour-bar regression catches all seven. In six
  of them the |U| colour bar's minimum is 0.026 to 0.16 m/s, against at most 0.0061 m/s in
  a normal run; which number the regression uses is not recorded.
- **Viscosity.** The model catches all eight; the ×100 and ×300 runs are not developed
  flows, and the ×300 run's colour bar ends at 359 m/s against a stated 28.4 m/s. The
  colour-bar regression catches two, ×0.1 and ×300.
- **Earlier states.** Steps 6000 and 7000, stated, temperature 0: 37 and 42 of 58. All 174
  stated frames: 123.
- **Variants.** At step 8000 the three stated variants agree on 41 of 58 frames. The
  temperature 0.7 answers call more normal runs `inconsistent` (20 and 16 of 29).
- The median answer took 4.00 s on the A6000; the 1,044 took 70 min.

Against October 30: there 31B was right for 67 of 80 frames and the peak rule for 56, with
eight frames of each of ten runs. Here, with one run as one sample and a regression
fitted on the same runs' answer key, the lead is gone.

A check outside the design: the largest Courant number after the first frame, with a
threshold between 0.43, the normal runs' largest, and 0.5, flags 10 of the 29 wrong runs
with no false alarm: the seven slip-wall runs, viscosity ×100 and ×300, inlet speed ×1.5.
It reads the solver log, not a picture, and the threshold was chosen after the counts.

## Limitations

- One model, one case and one window. The model's interval is 0.17 wide, the colour-bar
  regression's 0.21.
- The baselines read the colour-bar numbers that the pictures carry. A baseline on the
  arrays was not run and would see more.
- The cause score counts the profile runs' `inlet_speed` as wrong, though the visible
  effect of a half-closed inlet is a lower speed.
- The viscosity ×100 and ×300 states are not developed flows. They stay in the counts
  because the plan fixed the changes before the runs.
- The reasons were read by one AI agent, counting sentence patterns, with no second
  judge.
- The whole-run Courant check of October 30 does not apply here: 21 of the 29 normal
  runs go above 1 in their first steps, because each base's inlet speed differs from
  the start state's.
