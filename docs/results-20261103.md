In short: on 58 runs of the backward-facing step case, Gemma 4 31B told a wrongly set-up
run from a normal one 44 times. A logistic regression on the colour-bar numbers and the stated
setup did so 45 times. That is a tie, so this test does not show that the model does better than
the regression. The model finds more of the wrong runs, but it also calls many normal runs wrong. Its usual
reason is that the picture shows no step. The plotted window begins at the step's corner,
so no picture shows one.

The October 30 test asked whether a vision-language model can see that a run was set up
wrong. The lead of Gemma 4 31B over a simple rule there, the peak |U| rule, rested on two runs. This test asks the
question again with a design fixed before any request.

This test has 29 pairs of `pimpleFoam` runs of the step case. Each pair is one base setup
drawn at random, run as written and with one change. Each run gives three frames. Each
request sends the pictures of one frame with a short note. In the stated condition the note
gives the intended inlet speed and viscosity; in the blind condition it does not. Five
baselines answer from the same pictures and note: a constant baseline, the peak |U| rule of
October 30 and three logistic regressions.

By the plan, the conclusion that the model does better than the rules needs p < 0.05 in an
exact McNemar test against the baseline with most right answers. The comparison uses the last
frame of each run, in the stated condition, at the model's sampling temperature 0.

On these frames 31B is right for 44 of 58 runs and the baseline with most right answers
for 45. That baseline is a logistic regression on the colour-bar numbers and the stated setup.
The colour-bar numbers are the four numbers printed at the ends of the two colour bars. The
Design section gives the regression's inputs. The two disagree on 27 runs, 13 for the model and 14 for the baseline, and the
McNemar test gives p = 1.
The criterion is not met, so this test does not show that the model does better than the
rules.

The model and the baseline are right on different runs. The model catches 26 of the 29
wrong runs and the baseline 17. By kind of change, the model catches more of the speed,
viscosity and profile runs, and the baseline more of the slip-wall runs. The model answers
`inconsistent` on 11 of the 29 normal runs, the baseline on one. Nine of the eleven false
alarms say the picture shows no backward-facing step. The window begins at the step's corner,
so the step's face lies on the left edge and no picture shows it as an area.

In the blind condition the model answers `inconsistent` on 51 of 58 runs and is
right for 28. That is one below the constant baseline, which answers `consistent` on every run
and gets 29. Raising the model's sampling temperature from 0 to 0.7 changes 10 of the
58 stated answers with each of two seeds and lowers the number of right answers to 34 and
38.

## Design

- **Runs.** 30 base setups drawn at random with a fixed seed: inlet speed 20 to 40 m/s, kinematic viscosity
  1.0×10⁻⁵ to 2.5×10⁻⁵ m²/s, a start state of the developed flow at t = 0.06 to 0.1 s.
  Each base ran as written and with one change: inlet speed ×0.1 to ×1.5 in 8 pairs, viscosity ×0.1 to ×1000 in 9, `lowerWall` or both walls `slip` in 7, and the inlet profile in 6. The profile change sets the lowest quarter, half or three quarters of the inlet faces to velocity 0. The ×1000 viscosity run diverged,
  and its pair was dropped as the plan says: 29 pairs, 58 runs. A pilot of 13 other runs
  showed that a tilted inlet and a uniform start state leave no visible trace in the
  plotted window, so those kinds were dropped before the design was fixed.
- **Frames.** Steps 6000, 7000 and 8000 of each run. A frame is the two pictures, |U| and p, each
  drawn on its own colour scale in the solver's window. Step 8000 is the primary frame, the one the criterion uses. One run is one sample.
- **Request.** The system prompt describes the case and asks whether the pictures are
  consistent with it, with a `reason`, an `assessment` and a `cause`. Each request carries
  the two pictures, the system prompt and a short text note. Stated: the note gives the
  base's inlet speed and viscosity, the same text for both runs of a pair.
  Blind: it does not. Temperature 0 with one seed, and 0.7 with two seeds: six sets,
  1,044 requests, all to Gemma 4 31B as the 4-bit QAT Q4_0 file.
- **Scoring.** A normal run answered `consistent` or a wrong run `inconsistent` is right;
  `cannot_tell` is wrong on both. Right for the right reason adds that the cause named is
  the run's own change, `none` for a normal run. The statistical test is an exact McNemar
  test against the baseline with most right answers. The 95 % interval of the share right comes from a
  bootstrap over pairs. The criterion is p < 0.05 in the stated condition at temperature 0.
- **Baselines.** Five baselines read the same pictures and note, and none is given a normal run to compare with.
  The constant baseline answers `consistent` everywhere. The peak |U| rule of October 30
  calls a run wrong when the window's peak |U|, the colour bar's maximum, lies outside 1
  to 2 times the stated speed. Three logistic regressions are fitted while leaving one
  pair out at a time. One, the colour-bar regression, uses five inputs: the two |U| colour-bar numbers divided by the stated speed, the two p
  numbers divided by the square of the stated speed, and the logarithm of the stated
  viscosity. Another uses those five plus three
  pixel shares, and the last uses the pixel shares alone. The first pixel share is the part of the
  |U| picture drawn in the lowest 15 % of its colour scale, the slow flow. The second is how
  far that slow flow reaches along x in the lowest quarter of the picture, as a share of its
  width. The third is the part of the p picture drawn in the lowest 15 % of its colour scale.
- The request text cannot separate normal from wrong runs, because within a pair it is the same. The plan, the request set's manifest and the baselines were committed before the GPU jobs
  ([evidence](evidence/2026-11-03-wrong-setup-2/README.md)).

In the blind column only the constant and the pixels-only rows see what the model sees.
The other baselines read the stated numbers in both conditions, so their counts repeat.
The peak |U| rule needs the stated speed. The 95 % interval of the model's share of right
answers at temperature 0 is 0.67 to 0.84, the colour-bar regression's 0.67 to 0.88.

- **Right for the right reason**, stated, temperature 0: 27 of 58. These are the 18 normal runs and
  9 wrong runs, speed 7 and walls 2. No viscosity run gets the cause `viscosity`; the model
  names walls, other, inlet speed or inlet profile. All six profile runs get `inlet_speed`,
  with the colour bar's maximum below the stated speed as the reason. That is the peak |U|
  rule's comparison.
- **The false alarms.** Nine of the eleven reasons on normal runs say the picture shows
  no backward-facing step: "no step geometry (white area)", "no sudden expansion or
  separation zone". One sees checkerboarding in p, and one says only that the separation
  zone is missing.
  The sentence about the missing step also catches the inlet speed ×1.25 run. The 18 normal runs answered
  `consistent` all name a separation or recirculation zone behind a step.
- **Slip walls.** Three of the seven runs are answered `consistent` with "a recirculation
  zone and reattachment after a step". The colour-bar regression catches all seven. In six
  of them the |U| colour bar's minimum is 0.026 to 0.16 m/s, against at most 0.0061 m/s in
  a normal run. Which number the regression uses is not recorded.
- **Viscosity.** The model catches all eight. The ×100 and ×300 runs are not developed
  flows. The ×300 run's colour bar ends at 359 m/s against a stated 28.4 m/s. The
  colour-bar regression catches two, ×0.1 and ×300.
- **Earlier frames.** Steps 6000 and 7000, stated, temperature 0: 37 and 42 of 58 right.
  All 174 stated frames at temperature 0: 123 right.
- **Sets.** At step 8000 the three stated sets agree on 41 of 58 frames. The
  temperature 0.7 answers call 20 and 16 of the 29 normal runs `inconsistent`.
- The median answer took 4.00 s on the A6000; all 1,044 answers took 70 min.

On October 30, 31B was right for 67 of 80 frames and the peak |U| rule for 56, with
eight frames of each of ten runs. Here the lead is gone, with one run as one sample and a regression fitted
on the answer key of the other 28 pairs.

Outside the design, a threshold on the largest Courant number after the first frame flags
10 of the 29 wrong runs with no false alarm. The threshold lies between 0.43, the normal
runs' largest, and 0.5. It flags the seven slip-wall runs, viscosity ×100 and ×300, and inlet
speed ×1.5. It reads the solver log, not a picture, and the threshold was chosen after the counts.

## Limitations

- One model, one case and one window. The model's 95 % interval is 0.17 wide, the colour-bar
  regression's 0.21.
- The baselines read the colour-bar numbers that the pictures carry. A baseline on the
  arrays was not run and would see more.
- The cause score counts the profile runs' `inlet_speed` as wrong, though the visible
  effect of a half-closed inlet is a lower speed.
- The viscosity ×100 and ×300 states are not developed flows. They stay in the counts
  because the plan fixed the changes before the runs.
- The reasons were read by one AI agent, counting sentence patterns, with no second
  judge.
- The whole-run Courant check of October 30 does not apply here. In this test 21 of the 29 normal
  runs go above 1 in their first steps, because each base's inlet speed differs from
  the start state's.
